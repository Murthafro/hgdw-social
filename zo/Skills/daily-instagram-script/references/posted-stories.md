DAY 1 · MAY 14, 2026 · STORY / PHILOSOPHY
Golden Age — Birthday Edition
♪ Upbeat electronic instrumental — warm, optimistic, forward-moving

Most people turn 39 and make a wish. I made a 50-day plan.

I started on Wall Street. Built and lost a few companies. Had a manic episode at a family wedding. Then found myself in a garden in the Miami ghetto learning from mushrooms. And eventually — finally — figured out how to stop forcing and start flowing.

That’s the short version. The long version is 50 days.

Every day I’m going to tell you one story. What I learned about money, mental health, creativity, and joy — and how it all points to the same question:

Are you riding the right wave? Or are you still swimming upstream?

This is the golden age — where the future is fertile for those who choose to see it. I want you to see it.

Follow along. I’ll see you tomorrow.


DAY 2 · MAY 15, 2026 · JOY WAVE PHILOSOPHY
Joy Is a Frequency
♪ Dreamy synth, soft and expansive

Here’s the idea that changed everything for me.

Joy is not a reward. It’s not what happens when things go well. It’s not the thing you get to feel after you’ve earned it.

Joy is a frequency. A signal. Something you can tune into — or tune out of.

I started paying attention to when it shows up. The real kind, not the performed kind. A genuine giggle. A moment of being exactly where I’m supposed to be.

And I noticed: those moments weren’t random. They were pointing somewhere. Toward a direction. Toward a yes.

The Joy Wave is the practice of learning to read that signal — and riding it instead of overriding it.

That’s what this whole series is about. Not just the golden age out there. The golden age in here.

More on this tomorrow.


DAY 3 · MAY 16, 2026 · MINDSET
Functional Before Fun
♪ Warm lo-fi with a light pulse — playful but grounded

Third grade. My teacher caught me counting on my fingers.

She drew two giant hands on the chalkboard and had the whole class laugh at me.

I left that room with two beliefs: I’ll be great at math — and buried, quietly — I’m dumb.

That second one ran my life for years.

Until I meditated my way back to that room.

Turns out? I’m not dumb. That teacher was.

Also — I don’t even love math that much.

Resistance isn’t a stop sign. It’s just an old feeling sitting in your system.

Meet it with curiosity. Get your reps in anyway.

That’s how joy gets unlocked.

DAY 4 · MAY 17, 2026 · FERTILE — THEME INTRO
How I Lost My Feminine Side
♪ Warm acoustic, soft feminine energy

I spent years building like a man.

Not because anyone told me to — because it worked. At least for a while.

Wall Street. Startups. Hustle. Force. Build harder, push further, make it happen.

And I did make things happen. But something kept breaking — and it wasn’t the companies. It was me.

I didn’t know I’d lost my feminine side until I was sitting in Miami, completely depleted, wondering how someone this capable could feel this empty.

What I discovered over the next few years changed everything I thought I knew about how to build a life.

Over the next few weeks, I’m going to share three stories — about a company called the Matriarchy, about a garden in the Miami ghetto, and about a piece of land that kept teaching me the same lesson — that taught me the most important thing I know:

You can’t force what’s meant to flow.

DAY 7 · MAY 20, 2026 · PERSONAL STORY
🔥✨ THE MATRIARCHY ✨🔥
♪ Cinematic, building — emotional and grounded

VOICEOVER:
I actually tried to build the matriarchy.

Well, a company called The Matriarchy. It was a fintech platform for women and female founders to get capital and invest in each other and make money in the stock market. I was 30, backed by my ex-bosses, and every major VC in the country was calling. We got the name because my brilliant co-founder looked at me and said, ‘You are the matriarch. That’s what we have to call it.’ I looked at her like she was insane, and then I thought about it. It made me so uncomfortable that ten minutes later I was all in.

I had every dollar I had earned from my 9 years working 100 hours a week on Wall Street — and then some — in it. A seat at a table I had been working toward for years.

I sat down at the Gramercy Tavern. I was about to sign. And my whole body said no.

I walked away in tears. No plan. No idea what I was doing. I thought I had failed.

Someone said to me later: high achievers don’t burn out when something is hard. They burn out when something is wrong. I didn’t know that then. I just knew my body said no. And for the first time in my life, I listened to it.

Here’s what I learned on the other side of that table: the opposite of the patriarchy isn’t the matriarchy. It’s not about flipping the power structure. It’s something else entirely. Something older. Something the mycelium already knows.

Women aren’t just powerful. We’re channels. And when we actually listen — when we stop overriding the signal — we don’t just build differently. We build things that are actually alive.

I named that company after something I believed in before I understood it. I understand it now.
