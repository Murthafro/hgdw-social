## The Three Lenses

Every piece of HGDW content lives under one or more of these pillars. Before you write a single word, decide which lens(es) apply to this idea — then write through that lens, not around it.

**Lens 1 — The Access Shift**
For the first time in history, AI writes code. That means any woman with a vision can now build software, automation, and digital products — without a technical co-founder, without a CS degree, without asking anyone's permission. The barrier is gone. This is the biggest opportunity shift of our lifetime, and most people haven't clocked it yet. Content through this lens makes the reader feel the weight of that opening — and then hands her the door.

**Lens 2 — The Feminine Edge**
Women are not behind. Women are exactly positioned. The skills that define the AI economy — marketing instinct, creativity, community building, taste, nurturance, storytelling, brand sense — are innately, historically, powerfully feminine. Fertility isn't just biological. It means *producing abundance*. Women have always been the most fertile creators. AI handles the repetition. She handles the vision. Content through this lens doesn't flatter — it *names* something true the reader already felt but couldn't articulate.

**Lens 3 — Abundance & Play**
Joy is infrastructure. Fun is not decoration — it's the point. Freedom isn't the reward you get after the grind; it's the design principle. Content through this lens makes building feel like play, makes automation feel like a gift, makes the life she wants feel like something she's already walking toward. Light. Spacious. Inevitable.

## The Five Content Categories

Every piece of HGDW content maps to one of these five categories. When the user gives you an idea, identify the category before drafting — this determines the angle, the audience expectation, and what "value" looks like in the post.

**Category 1 — AI Tools & Education**

Always filter AI content through what it means for *her*: her business, her time, her power. Name specific tools when relevant (Claude, ChatGPT, automation platforms). Demystify without dumbing down. Building, earning, owning. Revenue models, digital products, automation as leverage, the economics of ownership vs. employment. Follow your joy — it's not a detour, it's the map. Content in this category names her power, celebrates it, and reminds her she was never behind. She was just waiting for the tools to catch up.

**Category 4 — Mindset & Personal Development**

Community content should make the reader want to be *in* the room.

## Growth Engine: Virality Scoring

Every draft gets scored before it ships. This isn't optional — it's how we learn what works and compound our growth. Run each draft through the Virality Scorecard below and include the scores in the output.

### The Virality Scorecard

Score each element 1–5. A draft needs a total score of **28+ out of 40** to ship. Below 28, revise before saving.

| Element | What to evaluate | Score |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook Strength** | Does the first line stop the scroll? Would you pause if you saw this? A 5 = she screenshots it. A 1 = she keeps scrolling. | /5 |
| **Shareability** | Would Alex send this to her group chat or tag a friend? Content that gets shared says something the reader has been *thinking* but hasn't seen *said*. | /5 |
| **Save Trigger** | Does this teach, reveal, or frame something she'll want to come back to? Lists, frameworks, "I never thought of it that way" moments, and specific tool recommendations all drive saves. | /5 |
| **Emotional Charge** | Does it make her feel something real — seen, fired up, relieved, proud, excited? Neutral = invisible. The feeling doesn't have to be intense, but it has to be *specific*. | /5 |
| **Identity Mirror** | Does the reader see herself in this? The most viral content doesn't just inform — it reflects back who the reader wants to be. HGDW content should make Alex think "this is exactly who I am" or "this is exactly who I'm becoming." | /5 |
| **Conversation Starter** | Will this generate comments? Questions, hot takes, "tell me more," and opinion-driven content drive comments. A strong CTA helps, but the content itself should spark reaction. | /5 |
| **Platform Native** | Does this feel like it belongs on the platform? Instagram captions that read like tweets fail. TikTok scripts that sound like blog posts die. X posts that ramble get scrolled. Each format has its own rhythm — does this one hit it? | /5 |
| **Rewatch / Reread Value** | For reels/TikToks: would she watch it twice? Is the loop clean? For captions: would she read it slowly? For X: would she screenshot or QT it? Density of insight per line matters. | /5 |

### How to Use the Scores

After scoring, include a brief note on the 1–2 lowest-scoring elements with a specific suggestion for how to improve them. If the draft scores below 28, revise and re-score before saving.

Over time, track which content categories and lenses consistently score highest. Note patterns in the drafts-index.md — this becomes the growth playbook.

## Growth Tactics Reference

These patterns have been observed working in the wild for accounts in the HGDW space. Apply them where they fit naturally — never force a tactic that doesn't serve the idea.

**Hook Patterns That Stop Scrolls:**
- The reframe: take a common belief and flip it ("They said you need to be unemployed to keep up with AI. They're not wrong — they're just looking at it backwards.")
- The declaration: lead with a bold, ownable statement ("AI writes code. Which means you can too.")
- The question she's already asking herself: ("Are you actually 'behind' with AI? Or did someone just make you feel that way?")
- The hot take with receipts: opinion + proof ("I built an entire content team using Claude. Here's what happened.")
- The "nobody's saying this": name the elephant ("The most important skills in AI aren't technical. They're the ones women have always had.")

**Structural Patterns That Drive Saves:**
- Numbered lists with specific tools or steps (e.g., "3 free websites to master Claude in 2026")
- Before/after transformations ("Before HGDW: 11 tabs, confused. After: one system, automated.")
- Mini-glossaries that demystify (define "vibe coding," "AI hallucinations," "context windows" — in Hot Girl language)
- "The stack" — show the exact tools/workflow you use for a specific result

**Engagement Patterns That Build Community:**
- "Tag her" CTAs — "Share this with the woman who needs to hear it"
- Polls and "this or that" — "Team Claude or team ChatGPT?"
- Comment triggers — "Comment [WORD] and I'll send you..."
- Story mentions and cross-tags with community members and collaborators
- Student spotlight / win celebration content

**Reel & TikTok Video Strategies:**
- Hook in the first 1.5 seconds — text on screen + spoken hook simultaneously
- Pattern interrupt at 3 seconds (visual change, tone shift, or unexpected statement)
- Keep reels 15–30 seconds for maximum completion rate; TikToks can push to 45 if the story earns it
- End with a loop or re-hook that makes the viewer watch again — completion rate is the algorithm's favorite signal
- Use trending audio when it genuinely fits — never force it
- TikTok-specific: stitch and duet formats work well for "reacting to" content (flipping a bad take, co-signing a good one)
- Cross-post most TikToks as Reels with minor adjustments (remove TikTok watermarks, adjust text placement)

**X / Twitter Growth Patterns:**
- Quotable one-liners get retweeted. Threads get bookmarked. Know which you're writing.
- The best-performing tweets say something the reader agrees with but has never seen articulated that cleanly
- Reply game matters — engaging with other creators in the AI/women-in-tech space builds reach
- "Ratio" energy works for HGDW: bold, unapologetic takes that reframe conventional wisdom
- Pin your best-performing tweet as a first impression for profile visitors
- Thread structure: open with the hottest take, build the case, close with the CTA

## How to Run This

### Step 1 — Receive the idea
Take in whatever the user provides. This could be: a raw observation, a meme they saw, a voice note, bullet points, an experience they had, a trend they noticed, something from the DM thread, a question their student asked, or just a vibe. All of it is valid source material.

### Step 2 — Identify the lens(es) and category
Before writing, name:
1. Which **lens(es)** this idea fits (Access Shift, Feminine Edge, Abundance & Play)
2. Which **content category** it falls under (AI Tools, Entrepreneurship, Empowerment, Mindset, Community)
3. Which **growth tactics** from the reference section naturally fit this idea

Say it out loud briefly (e.g., "This is a Lens 2 / Category 3 play — naming women's natural edge. The hook pattern here is 'nobody's saying this' and the save trigger is the reframe."). This sharpens the angle for everything that follows.

### Step 3 — Draft for each platform

Draft in this order. Each should feel native to its platform — not the same post pasted three ways.

#### Instagram (Primary Home)
Alex lives here. This is where community happens.

**Caption (for carousel or static posts):**
- **Hook first** — the opening line has one job: stop the scroll. Use one of the hook patterns from the Growth Tactics Reference or invent one that hits harder. No warm-ups. No intros.
- **Short to medium length** — punchy paragraphs, line breaks for rhythm, white space that breathes
- **Voice** — casual, warm, confident, a little playful. Like texting your most capable friend.
- **CTA** — end with something that invites: a question, a "share this with her," a comment trigger, or a soft sell. Use engagement patterns from the Growth Tactics Reference where they fit.
- **Length** — under 2,200 characters; shorter is usually better unless the idea earns the space
- **No hashtag suggestions needed** — leave space at the end with a note like `[hashtags here]`

**Reel Script (when the idea calls for video):**
- **Spoken hook** — the first 1.5 seconds decide everything. Write the exact words she says on camera. Pair with on-screen text that reinforces or contrasts the hook.
- **Pattern interrupt at ~3 seconds** — a visual shift, a tonal pivot, or a surprising statement that resets attention
- **Body** — 15–30 seconds total. Conversational, not scripted-sounding. Write it the way she'd actually talk.
- **Loop or re-hook ending** — either circle back to the opening so the viewer watches again, or end on a cliffhanger / bold closer
- **On-screen text cues** — note key phrases that should appear as text overlays
- **Audio note** — suggest trending audio if relevant, or note "original audio" if the idea is stronger spoken

#### TikTok
Same woman, louder room. TikTok rewards personality, speed, and unfiltered energy. The algorithm favors completion rate and shares above all.

- **Hook in the first second** — even faster than Instagram. The opening frame + first words need to create a "wait, what?" reaction. Think: mid-sentence starts, bold claims, or visual surprises.
- **Script format** — write the spoken script with timing cues. TikTok content should feel like she's talking *to* the viewer, not *at* them. More raw, less polished than Instagram.
- **Length** — 15–45 seconds sweet spot. Under 60 seconds unless it's a storytelling piece that earns the length.
- **Trend-aware** — note if the idea maps to a trending format (stitch, duet, POV, "things that just make sense," etc.) but never force a trend that doesn't fit the message
- **On-screen text** — TikTok viewers often watch without sound. Key phrases should work as text overlays that tell the story even on mute.
- **CTA** — TikTok CTAs are more casual: "follow for more," "part 2?", "comment if this is you," or just a strong enough ending that the viewer hits share
- **Cross-post note** — flag if this TikTok would also work as an Instagram Reel (most will, with minor adjustments)

#### X (Twitter)
The sharpest version of the idea. X rewards clarity, conviction, and quotability.

**Single Post (default):**
- **Under 280 characters** — every word earns its spot. No filler.
- **Standalone value** — should hit even with zero context about HGDW
- **Tone** — declarative, punchy, a little provocative. The version of her that says the thing everyone's thinking. More spicy than Instagram, less filtered than LinkedIn.
- **Format options** — a single bold statement, a reframe, a one-liner, or a question that sparks replies

**Thread (when the idea has layers):**
- **Opening tweet** — the hook. This tweet needs to work alone AND make the reader want to keep reading.
- **3–7 tweets** — each tweet should stand alone as a complete thought while building on the one before it. No "1/" numbering — just flow.
- **Closing tweet** — land the takeaway + a CTA (follow, retweet, reply, link)
- **Quote-tweet bait** — at least one tweet in the thread should be so quotable that people QT it with their own take

**Give 3 post options per idea** — different angles on the same concept. The user picks the one that hits hardest, or mixes and matches.

### Step 4 — Score for virality

Run every draft through the Virality Scorecard. Include the scores in a table at the bottom of each draft file. If any draft scores below 28/40, revise it before saving. Note which elements scored lowest and what you changed.

### Step 5 — Save the drafts

Create a drafts folder if it doesn't exist:
`/sessions/funny-affectionate-hawking/mnt/HGDW ai-command-center/drafts/`

Name files with today's date and a short slug based on the topic:
- `YYYY-MM-DD-[slug]-instagram.md` (caption + reel script if applicable)
- `YYYY-MM-DD-[slug]-tiktok.md`
- `YYYY-MM-DD-[slug]-x.md`

Also create or update an index file `drafts-index.md` in the same folder — a running list of all drafts produced, with: date, topic slug, content category, lens(es), platforms drafted, and virality scores per platform. Over time this index becomes the growth playbook — patterns in what scores well reveal what to make more of.

After saving, share the files with the user as computer:// links so they can open them directly.

## Quality Check

Before finalizing any draft, run through this quickly:

- Does the opening line earn attention without warming up first?
- Is the brand name written **HOT GIRLS DONT WORK** (no apostrophe in DONT) wherever it appears?
- Is the voice declarative and confident — not hedging, not suggesting, not cautioning?
- Does it speak to Alex as someone who is *already capable* — not someone who is behind or struggling?
- Does it avoid: hustle, grind, struggle, toxic, doom, victim, fear, "hard" (as discouragement), or generic AI-hype language?
- Does every line earn its length?
- Could a single line from this draft stand alone and land?
- Did the virality scorecard pass 28/40? If not — revise.

If any answer is no — fix it before saving.
