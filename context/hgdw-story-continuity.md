# HGDW Story Continuity Guide

This is the master reference for maintaining a cohesive brand narrative across all HGDW content. Every post, email, blog, and video should advance the brand story — not just exist in isolation.

---

## The Brand Story Arc (from the Story Bible)

HGDW tells ONE story across all content. Every piece is a chapter.

**The arc:**
She's grinding → She finds HGDW → She builds something real → She closes the laptop → She runs her life from her phone → She's not behind, she's early → She's free.

### The 10 Narrative Beats

Every piece of content maps to one of these beats. They're not linear — you cycle through them, revisiting beats at different depths.

| Beat | Story Moment | Content Translation | Scene |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Grind** | She's overwhelmed, trading alive hours for tasks she resents | "You're too hot to be working this hard" — the diagnosis | Scene 1 |
| **2. The Wrong Room** | She tries an AI class. Too bro-coded, over her head. Closes laptop. | Origin story: "I tried to sign my girlfriends up for an AI class..." | Scene 2 |
| **3. The Door Opens** | She finds HGDW. Her language. Her aesthetic. Her room. | "We built a room for you" — brand intro, welcome content | Scene 3 |
| **4. The First Win** | She builds something real. It's fast. It sounds like HER. | Demo content, testimonials, "she built a website in an afternoon" | Scene 4 |
| **5. Off the Laptop** | She realizes she doesn't need a computer. Phone only. | "I closed my laptop and never looked back" — the reveal | Scene 5 |
| **6. The Party Scene** | Mid-party, phone buzzes, she answers her agent, keeps talking. Friends are floored. | The most shareable visual: AI as text thread, not task list | Scene 6 |
| **7. Life on Agents** | Her WHOLE life runs on agents. School, errands, inbox, side hustle. All from her phone. | Day-in-the-life content. Zero computer shots. | Scene 7 |
| **8. The Opportunity** | The golden age is opening. She can walk through or keep grinding. | Urgency/invitation content, "the door is open now" | Scene 8 |
| **9. The Inversion** | "I'm not behind. I'm early." The fear inverts. | Contrarian hook content, "AI won't take your job..." | Scene 9 |
| **10. The Resolution** | Tuesday afternoon. Yacht/garden/beach/kids. Phone buzzes. She answers. Goes back to her life. | "Work less. ___ more." — the recurring series | Scene 10 |

## The 5 Narrative Threads

These are the ongoing stories that run underneath all content. Every post should advance at least one thread:

### Thread 1: THE TRANSFORMATION
**What it tracks:** Alex's journey from overwhelmed to free
**Key progression:** Doesn't know AI → Tries HGDW → First win → Closes laptop → Runs life from phone
**Content markers:** Before/after, struggle stories, "I used to..." reveals, transformation testimonials
**Never:** Show the transformation as complete. She's always evolving. Always finding new "mores."

### Thread 2: THE COMMUNITY
**What it tracks:** The squad effect — hot girls bringing their friends
**Key progression:** Solo learner → Party scene → Friends sign up → Movement
**Content markers:** DMs, girlfriend texts, "I sent this to my friend," shared wins, squad moments
**Never:** Make it feel exclusive or cliquey. The door is always open.

### Thread 3: THE TOOLS
**What it tracks:** What AI can actually DO — the practical layer
**Key progression:** Scary/confusing → Simple/fun → Invisible/background → "My life runs itself"
**Content markers:** Tool demos, "watch me do this in 2 minutes," new tool discoveries, comparisons
**Never:** Make tools the point. They're the mechanism, not the product. The product is time.

### Thread 4: THE REBELLION
**What it tracks:** The anti-grind, anti-hustle, anti-system energy
**Key progression:** "The system wasn't built for me" → "I'm building my own" → "I'm not behind, I'm early"
**Content markers:** Contrarian takes, industry callouts, "every other AI class sells skills — we sell time"
**Never:** Be angry or bitter. The energy is playful defiance, not resentment.

### Thread 5: THE GOLDEN AGE
**What it tracks:** The bigger picture — we are at a historic inflection point
**Key progression:** AI is happening → Most people don't see it → She sees it → She's early → She's inviting others
**Content markers:** Lab announcements translated, "this just changed everything," future-casting, trend alerts
**Never:** Create FOMO or urgency that implies she's late. She's EARLY. Always.

## Continuity Rules

### 1. Every post advances a thread
Before publishing, the Content Chief must tag which thread(s) the post advances. If it doesn't advance any thread — it's filler. Hold it.

### 2. No thread goes dormant for more than 2 weeks
If the story log shows Thread 2 (Community) hasn't been touched in 14 days, prioritize it in the next content plan.

### 3. Callbacks create cohesion
Reference previous content explicitly: "Remember when I showed you [X]? Here's what happened since." This turns isolated posts into episodes.

### 4. Recurring series are thread anchors
Each thread should have at least one recurring series:
- Thread 1: "Before AI / After AI" series
- Thread 2: "My girlfriend just texted me..." series
- Thread 3: "Watch me build this in [X] minutes" series
- Thread 4: "Things they don't tell you about AI" series
- Thread 5: "Work Less. ___ More." series (the mad-libs tagline — swap the verb each time)

### 5. The visual identity evolves with the story
- Beats 1-3: Laptop, desk, grind imagery is OK
- Beats 4-5: Transition — laptop closing, phone appearing
- Beats 6-10: NO COMPUTER ON SCREEN. Phone, text bubbles, woman somewhere alive.

### 6. The emotional arc matters more than the topic
Two posts about the same AI tool can serve different narrative purposes:
- "This tool saved me 3 hours" = Thread 3 (Tools) + Beat 4 (First Win)
- "My girlfriend just tried this tool and texted me at midnight" = Thread 2 (Community) + Beat 6 (Party Scene)

## Theme Tracking Categories

Tag every post with these categories for pattern analysis:

**Theme:** The topic area (AI tools, automation, time freedom, community, mindset, lifestyle, behind-the-scenes)

**Emotional register:** The feeling the post creates (inspiration, recognition, excitement, defiance, warmth, humor, urgency)

**Story position:** Where in the arc this lands (early = grind/discovery, middle = transformation/wins, late = freedom/invitation)

**Audience entry point:** Which Alex does this speak to?
- **Cold Alex:** Never heard of HGDW. Needs Beat 1-3 content.
- **Warm Alex:** Follows but hasn't bought. Needs Beat 4-6 content.
- **Hot Alex:** Customer or about to be. Needs Beat 7-10 content.

## Anti-Patterns to Catch

The story tracker should flag these:

1. **Thread starvation** — Any thread untouched for 14+ days
2. **Beat clustering** — Too many posts on the same beat (e.g., all "first win" demos, no rebellion)
3. **Tone drift** — Posts that feel corporate, heavy, or jargon-y (voice check against brand-voice.md)
4. **Missing callbacks** — No references to previous content in the last 2 weeks
5. **Laptop creep** — Computer/desk imagery appearing in late-arc content (Beats 6-10)
6. **Skill-heavy imbalance** — All teaching, no story or passion (Part 11 of the workbook)
7. **Convert without context** — Selling before building enough Attract/Nurture/Position
8. **Generic hooks** — Diagnosis-first instead of symptom-first messaging (Part 12 of workbook)
