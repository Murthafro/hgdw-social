## Creative Director Idols / North Stars

> These are the creative directors Courtney models herself after. They define the tier she is building toward.

- **Pharrell Williams** — Multi-hyphenate creative director, musician, and cultural architect. Called in by Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Adidas. Doesn't just make things look good — he shifts what's *possible*.
- **Virgil Abloh** — Designer, DJ, founder of Off-White. Brought street culture into luxury. Worked across fashion, furniture, music, and art simultaneously. Defined a generation.
- **Es Devlin** — Stage and set designer for Beyoncé, Adele, U2, the Super Bowl, the Met Gala. Creates immersive worlds that transform how people *feel* in a space.

> What they have in common: they are called in at the highest level, given creative authority, and trusted to shape the soul of a project — not just the surface. They don't blend in — they define the era.

## My Ideal Clients / Audiences

Courtney works across two client types:

---

### Client Type 1: The Operator Partner (Equity)

- **Who they are:**
  Multi-exited founders and elite operators — brilliant executors who are deeply in the details and the weeds of building companies. They partner with visionaries and creative women because they know what they can't do: see the big picture, access top-tier creativity, or make something feel *cool*. They are not creative. They are not cool. They need Courtney for that.

- **In one sentence:**
  The person who can build the machine but can't dream the dream — or sell it.

- **Where they are in their journey:**
  Running or scaling a company, looking for a creative partner who can come in at the top — not to execute, but to *elevate*. They're open to equity partnerships with the right creative director.

---

### Client Type 2: Corporate & Product Partners (Project-Based)

- **Who they are:**
  Established companies, brands, and product partners that hire Courtney for specific creative projects — campaigns, brand direction, experiential concepts, launches, activations. Think: the brand that needs a visionary brought in to make a moment, not just a deck.

- **In one sentence:**
  The brand that knows they need something extraordinary and is willing to pay for the person who can deliver it.

- **Where they are in their journey:**
  They have the resources and infrastructure. What they're missing is the spark — the cultural relevance, the creative angle, the unexpected idea that makes people pay attention.

- **The ask:**
  Creative direction for a specific project, campaign, product launch, or brand moment. Paid engagements, not equity.

---

## What They Struggle With

> Shared pain points across both client types:

1. **"I can build it, but I can't sell the vision."**
   Strong operational or product skills, but no ability to articulate a compelling story or make investors, customers, and talent *feel* something.

2. **"Our brand doesn't reflect what we actually are."**
   The company or product is great, but it doesn't *look* or *feel* great. The creative direction is missing, inconsistent, or just not cool.

3. **"We don't know how to make this go viral — or even get noticed."**
   Not plugged into culture. Can't manufacture virality or create content that resonates. Need someone who is.

4. **"We need a big idea — something that makes people stop and pay attention."**
   (Especially for corporate/product partners) They have a launch, a campaign, or a moment coming. They need a visionary to make it extraordinary.

---

## What They Want (The Ask)

1. A visionary Creative Director who owns the brand, the vibe, and the story — not just consults on it.
2. Access to top-tier creativity and cultural relevance they can't generate themselves.
3. A partner who brings the "cool" — so they don't have to figure out what that even means.
4. For corporates: a specific creative concept, campaign, or direction delivered at the highest level.

## Decision Triggers

- **What makes them say YES:**
  They *see* her. Her brand, her presence, her results speak before she does. They approach her — not the other way around. The signal is: "She's the one. She has what we don't."

- **What makes them say NO:**
  If she seems like a vendor or freelancer rather than a partner. If she's chasing them. If the creative work feels generic or trend-following rather than visionary.

---

## Language & Tone They Respond To

- **They love:** Vision, clarity, cultural authority, beauty with purpose, boldness, results
- **They hate:** Fluff, hype, anything that feels like it's trying too hard, vague "creative" promises without substance
- **Words that resonate:** Vision, direction, equity, brand, story, magnetic, curated, elevated
- **Words that repel:** Freelance, consulting, package, retainer, deliverables, hustle
