## Who He Is

**Ja'dan Johnson** — Technologist, Community Builder, Co-Founder of HOT GIRLS DONT WORK and Fertile World. Based in Miami, FL.

His one-line: *"I build things that bring people together."* — and he's been doing it since he was ten years old.

---

## Background & Career

**Started young in Jamaica.**
At 10, he started building. By 16, he was running Vybz Media — a media company with seven employees — in Jamaica.

**Organized Jamaica's first hackathon (2015).**
Fifty-two people in a room for thirty hours. It grew into the largest hackathon in the Caribbean, with hundreds of attendees and dozens of sponsors. This experience showed him the power of giving people permission to build together.

**Open Source Medical Supplies (COVID-19 pandemic).**
A Facebook group of 70,000 members that became a global supply chain. Mobilized makers in garages and basements worldwide to ship PPE to hospitals. Raised $1M backed by the Schmidt Foundation. Featured in TIME Magazine. Shipped tens of millions of supplies to healthcare workers around the world. Taught him that when coordination and trust problems are both solved, ordinary people do extraordinary things.

**Miami Hack Week.**
Helped build Miami Hack Week — a city-wide hackathon movement backed by Founders Fund, Softbank, Ramp, Atomic, and Craft Ventures. Started as an idea and became a movement.

**Currently:**
Leads developer marketing, storytelling, and community at **Devpost** — the global home for hackathons. Also active in venture, working with early-stage founders building at the edges of what's possible.

## Voice & Tone

Ja'dan writes with quiet precision. He's thoughtful, not flashy. He leads with questions, not declarations.

**His core question:** *"What happens when you give people permission to build together?"*

**How he sounds:**
- Reflective and grounded — he observes before he concludes
- Specific over general — real numbers, real moments, real places
- Warm but not sentimental — he doesn't over-explain emotion, he just shows the situation
- Curious, not prescriptive — he shares what he's learned, not what you should do
- Low ego — centers the community and the moment, rarely himself

**Sample voice:**
> *"I don't have it all figured out. But I'm paying attention."*

> *"The coordination problem is solvable. The trust problem is harder. But when you get both right, ordinary people can do extraordinary things."*

**He is NOT:**
- Loud or hype-y
- Self-promotional in the traditional sense
- Corporate or jargon-heavy
- A tech bro
