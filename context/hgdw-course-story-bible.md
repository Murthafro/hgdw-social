# Hot Girls Don't Work (AI Course) — Story Bible
*Brand narrative framework based on McKee's Story principles*

---

## Premise

**Logline:** AI won't take your job — but a hot girl who uses it will stop letting her job take her life.

**Controlling Idea / Theme:** When a woman stops automating the wrong things (her dreams, her time, her potential) and starts automating the right things (the boring, the repetitive, the soul-draining), she gets her time back — and time is hers to spend however she wants.

**The Promise:** HOT GIRLS DONT WORK. We use AI. Work Less. [Dance / Travel / Create / Build / Garden / Paint / Mother / Cook / Beach / Engineer / Earn / Play] More. The verb is whatever she wants more of in her life — not ours to assign.

**The Core Differentiator — We Sell Time, Not Skills:** Every other AI course sells skill acquisition: prompt engineering, productivity hacks, "10 tools to 10x your output." That's a transaction — learn this, get better at work. HGDW sells the thing skill was always supposed to buy in the first place: time. AI is the mechanism. Time is the product. We don't ask "what can AI help you do better at work" — we ask "what do you actually want more of in your life," and then we hand her the tool to take it back.

**Central Value at Stake:** Freedom — specifically, a woman's right to her own time, and the right to spend it on whatever actually makes her life hers.

**Story ends on:** Triumphant positive. She's early to the golden age, not late to the game — and she's spending her reclaimed time on her own terms.

---

## The World

**Period:** Now — the inflection point where AI shifts from "tech bro tool" to mass-market superpower.

**Duration:** The story spans a transformation arc — from overwhelmed dabbler to confident AI superuser.

**Location:** Everywhere women work, grind, and wish they were somewhere else. The 9-to-5. The laptop at midnight. The group chat where everyone's exhausted.

**How characters live/work:** Working harder than they want to. Trading time for money. Running on a treadmill they didn't design and can't get off.

**Power dynamics:** Society was built for industrialization — for the factory floor, the male breadwinner, the 40-hour week. Women have been trying to fit into that mold ever since. The tech world inherited those rules: AI tools have been coded, marketed, and taught by and for a specific kind of person. She is not that person. She has always been playing an away game.

**What's original:** This is the first story where AI isn't the future of work — it's the exit from work. Where "learn AI" isn't a survival strategy — it's a lifestyle upgrade. Where the woman who was supposed to be left behind is actually the first one through the door. And unlike every other AI brand, the product isn't the skill — it's the time the skill buys back, spent however she wants.

**The Villain:** The industrial-age work model. A system built for factories, not for women with kids, taste, style, and genuinely better things to do with their time.

**The Negation of the Negation (darkest version):** She keeps grinding for 10 more years. AI transforms every industry. She never got on the right side of it — not because she couldn't, but because no one ever taught it to her in a way that felt like *her.* The cost isn't just time. It's the life she could have been living.

---

## Protagonist

**Name:** Every Woman.

**Characterization (surface):** Ages 25–55. Mom, entrepreneur, employee, freelancer. She shows up in all forms — the one who's overwhelmed and doesn't know where to start, and the one who's fired up and ready to go all-in. What unites them isn't their job title. It's the feeling: *this world wasn't designed for me.*

**Conscious Want:** To learn how to use AI. Practically. Without it going over her head.

**Unconscious Need:** To feel seen. To know she belongs in the room. To stop apologizing for not speaking a language nobody taught her. She doesn't need to become a tech person — she needs someone to translate the tech into *her* language.

**Fatal Flaw / Wound:** She believes she's behind. The message she's absorbed — from tech culture, from hustle culture, from a world built for someone else — is that AI is complicated, that she's not a "tech person," and that the window may already be closing. This belief keeps her on the treadmill.

**True Character:** Under pressure (when she actually tries it, when it actually works), she is a builder. A creator. A woman with taste and vision who was just waiting for the right tool. Her creativity isn't behind her genius — it *is* her genius.

**She is too hot to be working this hard.** Her values are her kids, her style, her fun, her freedom. Work is the thing in the way. AI is the thing that moves it.

---

## Forces of Antagonism

**Inner:** The belief that she's not a tech person. That this isn't for her. That she's too behind to catch up.

**Personal:** The job, the inbox, the to-do list, the grind — all the boring tasks eating her most alive hours.

**Social:** A work culture built on hustle as virtue. "The harder you work, the more you deserve." She's been rewarded for suffering.

**Environmental:** AI tools built for, marketed to, and taught by tech bros. Every tutorial that made her feel stupid. Every class that went straight over her head.

**Primary antagonistic force:** The industrial-age work model and the tech world that inherited its aesthetic — making her feel like an outsider in the one room that could actually set her free.

**Why the protagonist is an underdog:** She's playing by rules she didn't write, in a game she didn't design, with tools that were never built with her in mind. Every signal she's received tells her this isn't for her.

---

## Story Structure

**Unity spine:** Because she discovered that AI was actually built for her (once someone translated it), she finally stopped working like an industrial-age drone and started living like a woman with taste, time, and options.

**Genre:** Transformation story / Empowerment origin story / Underdog triumph

**The Elle Woods Principle:** She doesn't become a different kind of person to win. She wins on her own terms, in her own aesthetic, bringing her whole squad with her.

---

## Inciting Incident

**The brand origin:** Courtney tries to sign her girlfriends up for an AI class. It's over their heads — bro-coded, technical, built for someone else entirely. They all come back and say: *you teach us.* HOT GIRLS DONT WORK is born.

**The student's inciting incident:** She finds HGDW. A room that was built for *her* — her language, her aesthetic, her life. For the first time, AI doesn't feel like a threat or a foreign country. It feels like a cheat code with her name on it.

**Dramatic question raised:** Can a woman who doesn't see herself as "tech savvy" actually use AI to transform her work and her life — on her own terms?

**Answer at the climax:** She's not behind. She's *early.* And yes.

---

## Progressive Complications

### Turning Point 1 — The First Win
**Action taken:** She builds something real. A website, in her own voice, with AI doing the heavy lifting.

**The Gap:** She expected it to be hard, technical, confusing. It wasn't. It was fast. It sounded like *her.*

**What shifts:** The limiting belief cracks. "I can automate my work" isn't a concept anymore — it's a lived experience. She's not watching someone else do this. She did it herself.

**Why there's no going back:** She has proof now. Her own proof. You can't un-know what you know.

---

### Turning Point 1.5 — Off the Laptop, Onto the Phone
**Action taken:** She realizes she doesn't need to sit at a computer to use AI. She starts managing her agent from her phone — checking in, answering a quick question, redirecting it — the same way she'd text a friend.

**The Gap:** She expected AI to mean more screen time. Instead it means less. She closes the laptop and walks outside.

**What shifts:** The tool disappears into the background. It stops looking like "doing AI" and starts looking like texting someone who happens to get things done for her.

---

### Turning Point 2 — The Identity Shift (The Party Scene)
**Action taken:** She's at a party with her girlfriends. Mid-conversation, her phone buzzes — her agent has a question. She answers in one line without missing a beat and goes right back to the party.

**The Gap:** Her girlfriends watch this happen in real time and are floored. She isn't "at work." She's at a party. And work is still getting done.

**What shifts:** She stops being a student and becomes someone who runs her life through agents — visibly, casually, in front of her community. This is the Elle Woods moment: she doesn't win alone, and she doesn't win quietly. Her girlfriends start texting their own agents by the end of the night.

**Why there's no going back:** She has proof, witnessed by people she loves, that work doesn't require her presence anymore. She can never again believe she has to be at a desk for things to get done.

---

### Turning Point 3 — Her Whole Life Runs on Agents
**Action taken:** She organizes everything through agents — not just work tasks, but school pickups, meal planning, errands, her side hustle, her inbox. She runs all of it from her phone: in the grocery line, at her kid's practice, on the beach.

**The Gap:** She realizes the scope was always bigger than she thought. This isn't a work tool. This is a *life* tool — and she is never at a computer anymore.

**What shifts:** She crosses from "I use AI for work" to "my life runs itself — I just answer texts." The old way doesn't just feel hard — it feels genuinely absurd. Why would she ever sit at a desk again?

**Why there's no going back:** The treadmill she was on didn't just slow down. She stepped off it entirely, and she did it from a beach chair.

---

## The "Crisis" — The Opportunity

This story doesn't have a traditional dark night of the soul. The moment of choice isn't a dilemma — it's a door.

**The realization:** The golden age is opening. AI is reshaping every industry, every job, every way of working. She is standing at the threshold right now. She can walk through — or she can keep doing things the hard way.

**The two paths:**
- Step into the golden age: automate the boring, step into her genius, design her life.
- Keep grinding: do it the old way, the hard way, the way that was never built for her.

**There is no villain holding the door shut.** The only thing that keeps her on the treadmill is not knowing the door was there.

**She's not behind. She's early.**

---

## Climax & Resolution

**The climax:** The realization lands. *I'm not catching up. I'm ahead.* She got here before almost everyone else. The fear she walked in with — "AI is going to take my job, I'm behind" — inverts completely. She's on the right side of it. She always could have been.

**What the climax reveals about her true character:** She was never behind. She was in the wrong classroom. Once someone built the right room for her, she ran.

**Resolution — her life on a Tuesday:**
She has her time back. What she does with it is entirely hers to choose — and that's the point. It might be:
- Earn More (a new income stream she built with AI)
- Dance More. Travel More. Create More. Build More. Garden More. Paint More. Mother More. Cook More. Beach More. Engineer More.
- Or simply: the overwhelm is gone, and she has systems, clarity, and breathing room to figure out what "more" means for her

The resolution is never "and now she works less and earns more." It's "and now she works less, and does *whatever she wants* more." Income is one possible answer among dozens — never the default, never the assumed finish line.

**The closing image:** A woman on a yacht, in her garden, at the beach, on the floor with her kids — wherever she actually wants to be, laptop closed or nowhere in sight. Her phone buzzes. It's her agent, not her boss, not a deadline. She glances down, answers in a few words the way she'd answer a girlfriend's text, and goes right back to her life. The work is happening somewhere else, without her, because she built it that way.

**Visual identity note:** The computer should almost disappear from the brand's imagery past the early "first win" beat. The signature visual is a phone, a text bubble, and a woman somewhere alive. That image *is* the proof of "we sell time, not skills" — show, don't tell.

**The closing line:** *"It's easier than you think. And it's awesome."*

## Dialogue & Voice

**The text she sends her girlfriend at 11pm:** *"It's easier than you think & awesome."*

**What she's really saying:** I was scared too. It wasn't what I expected. You should come.

**The other text — to her agent, mid-party, mid-vacation, mid-everything:** Short. Casual. The same register she'd use with a friend — not a command, not a prompt, just a quick reply. This is a key voice signature: she never talks to her agent like she's operating a machine. She talks to it like she's managing a very competent assistant who happens to live in her phone.

**Voice notes:** Elle Woods energy throughout. Unapologetic femininity. Smart without performing smartness. She doesn't explain herself — she just wins and invites you to come along.

The brand voice never hedges. Never apologizes. Never positions AI as complicated or intimidating. It's always *obviously for her, obviously fun, obviously worth it.*

## Exposition Strategy

**Key backstory fact #1 — Society was built for industrialization, not for her.**
Revealed through: the frustration she already feels before she finds HGDW. We don't lecture about history — we just show her on the treadmill and let her recognize herself.

**Key backstory fact #2 — Every AI class before this went over their heads.**
Revealed through: the brand origin story. Courtney tried to sign her girlfriends up for an AI class. It failed. They came back and said: *you teach us.* HGDW exists because the alternative didn't work.

**The Secret:** She was never behind. She was just in the wrong room. The moment someone built the right room for her, she didn't just catch up — she flew.

## Unresolved Challenges / Notes for Development

- The course has two entry points (overwhelmed dabbler vs. ready to go all-in) — the story needs to feel true for both without being split into two different narratives. The solution: make the *feeling* of being seen the universal entry point. Both women feel unseen by every other AI resource. HGDW sees them.
- The transformation has three possible endpoints (income, time, reduced overwhelm) — the marketing story should honor all three as equally valid wins, not rank them.
- Elle Woods didn't just win — she changed the room. Consider how HGDW positions not just individual transformation but community transformation (the party scene, the girlfriends, the squad).
