# Hot Girls Don't Work (AI Course) — Scene Outline
*Brand story step-outline | Use this as a living reference for sales pages, scripts, and content*

---

**Instructions for use:** Each scene is the story beat that powers a piece of content, a section of your sales page, or a script. Scenes can be filmed, written, or designed — this is your content architecture, not just a narrative document. Add, reorder, or expand scenes as the brand deepens.

---

## Act One — The World She's In

| # | Scene | Structural Role |
|---|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | She's grinding. Overwhelmed, exhausted, trading her most alive hours for tasks she resents. Her real values — kids, style, fun, freedom — are waiting on the other side of a to-do list that never ends. | Opening / Establish the world and the wound |
| 2 | AI arrives. The message is clear: this is the future. The unspoken message is also clear: this wasn't built for you. She tries a class. It goes over her head. She closes the laptop. | The false entry — the world that doesn't work for her |
| 3 | She finds HGDW. For the first time, someone is speaking her language. The aesthetic, the tone, the vibe — it's hers. She doesn't have to become a different person to walk in. | **Inciting Incident** — the door opens |

---

## Act Two — The Transformation

| # | Scene | Structural Role |
|---|-------|-----------------|
| 4 | She tries it. Builds a website, in her own voice, with AI doing the work. It's fast. It sounds like *her.* She expected complicated. She got easy. | **Turning Point 1** — "I can automate my work" / The first crack in the limiting belief |
| 5 | She realizes she doesn't have to be at the computer at all. Her agent is handling the thing — she's checking in by text, from her phone, like she's texting a friend. She closes the laptop and walks outside. | Progressive complication — the shift from "using a tool" to "managing an agent" |
| 6 | She's at a party. Her girlfriends are there. Mid-conversation, her phone buzzes — her agent has a question, she answers in one line without missing a beat, and goes right back to the party. Her girlfriends watch, stunned. They start texting their own agents by the end of the night. | **Turning Point 2** — Identity shift / "I'm not at my desk. I'm not even thinking about work. And it's still getting done." The Elle Woods moment. |
| 7 | She organizes her whole life around agents — not just work. School pickups, meal planning, errands, her side hustle, her inbox. She runs all of it from her phone, in line at the grocery store, at her kid's practice. She is never at a computer. | **Turning Point 3** — The point of no return / "My life runs itself — I just have to answer texts" |

---

## Act Three — The Realization

| # | Scene | Structural Role |
|---|-------|-----------------|
| 8 | She pauses. Takes stock. The golden age is opening — AI is reshaping everything, every industry, every way of working. She's standing at the door right now. She can walk through, or she can keep doing things the hard way. | The Opportunity — the choice made visible |
| 9 | The fear she walked in with inverts. She came in thinking: *I'm behind, AI is going to take my job.* She leaves knowing: *I'm early. I'm on the right side of it. I always could have been — I just needed the right room.* | **Climax** — The identity reversal / "I'm not behind. I'm early." |
| 10 | A Tuesday afternoon — on a yacht, in the garden, at the beach, on the floor with her kids, wherever she actually wants to be. Her laptop is closed or not even in the room. Her phone buzzes, she glances down, answers her agent in a few words the way she'd answer a girlfriend's text, and goes right back to her life. The work is happening somewhere else, without her. | **Resolution** — The after / What "winning" looks like, and it's hers to define |

---

## The Closing Image

She's somewhere good — a yacht, a garden, the beach, the floor with her kids. Her phone buzzes. It's her agent, not her boss. She answers in a few words, the same way she'd answer a girlfriend, and goes back to her life.

Then she texts an actual girlfriend: *"It's easier than you think. And it's awesome."*

Her girlfriend signs up.

## Content Map — How Scenes Become Videos

| Scene | Content angle |
|-------|--------------|
| 1 | "You're too hot to be working this hard." Hook — the diagnosis |
| 2 | "I tried to sign my girlfriends up for an AI class. It went over their heads." — origin story reel |
| 3 | "We built a room for you." — HGDW brand intro |
| 4 | "She built a website in an afternoon." — first win testimonial / demo content |
| 5 | "I closed my laptop and never looked back" — the phone-not-laptop reveal, the moment work stops looking like work |
| 6 | The party scene — phone buzzes mid-conversation, she answers her agent in one line, keeps talking to her friends. This is the single most shareable visual in the whole brand: AI handled like a text thread, not a task list. |
| 7 | "My agents run my life" — day-in-the-life content: school pickup, grocery line, kid's practice, all narrated over texts to her agent. Zero shots of a computer. |
| 8 | "The golden age is opening. You can step in now or keep doing it the hard way." — urgency/invitation |
| 9 | "AI isn't going to take your job. A hot girl who uses AI will." — contrarian hook, most viral potential |
| 10 | "We use AI. Work less. ___ more." — the mad-libs tagline format, paired with the yacht/garden/beach/kids visual and a phone buzz. Run this as a recurring content series, swapping the verb each time (Dance More / Travel More / Garden More / Mother More / Build More / Earn More) to hit different segments of the audience without ever pinning the brand to one definition of "more." |

**Note on visual identity:** The computer should almost never be on screen past Scene 5. The brand's signature image is a phone, a text bubble, and a woman somewhere alive — not a laptop and a desk. That's the visual proof of the "we sell time, not skills" claim.

## The Differentiation Engine

This is the line that should anchor paid ads, the sales page hero section, and any "why us" content:

> **Every other AI class sells you a skill. We sell you your time back.**

Use this contrast directly in content: show what a typical AI course promises (productivity, output, "10x your work") next to what HGDW promises (your actual life, on your actual terms). The gap between those two promises is the entire brand moat.
